Fountain solution keeps the non-image areas of an offset plate clean. But most of what goes into the dampening system is not the concentrate. It is the water it is diluted with. That is why two printers using the same fountain solution can see very different results on press, and why choosing a grade starts with your incoming water.

## What fountain solution does

Fountain solution, also called dampening solution, fount or fountain etch, dampens the lithographic plate before it contacts the inking rollers. The non-image areas accept the water film and reject ink, so only the image prints. The concentrate is diluted with water at the press, and the working solution has to stay balanced through the whole run: enough water to keep the plate clean, but no more than the job needs.

## Why water hardness matters

Hard water carries dissolved minerals, mainly calcium and magnesium salts. In a dampening system these minerals do not disappear. Over time they can:

- build up as calcium deposits on dampening and inking rollers, which makes rollers lose their ability to carry ink and water evenly
- work against the buffer in the fountain solution, making pH harder to hold steady
- make it harder to run with reduced water settings, which affects colour density and drying

Soft water behaves differently and needs a different balance in the concentrate. A formulation designed for one water type will not perform at its best with the other.

Water hardness is not fixed. It differs between mains supply and well water, and between areas. If your press room changes water source, it is worth checking again.

## How to check your incoming water

You do not need a laboratory to get a useful answer:

1. **Ask your supplier.** Water boards and treatment suppliers can usually give a hardness figure for your area.
2. **Use a test kit.** Water hardness test strips or drop kits give a quick reading at the tap that feeds the press.
3. **Look at your press.** Persistent calcium build-up on rollers is a strong sign of hard water.

If you are not sure, tell our technical team your water source and press, and we can help you decide.

## Getting the dilution right

Each grade is a one-step concentrate: dilute it with water to the recommended strength, and the gum, alcohol substitute and other working ingredients are already in the mix. Our team can give you a starting dilution for your press, plates and water.

Once the press is running, good practice is to:

- check the working solution regularly, so changes are caught before they show up on the sheet
- keep the dampening system and its tanks clean, because dirt and paper dust shorten the life of the solution
- change the solution on a schedule instead of only topping it up

If scumming appears, our [plate gum](../../products/plate-gum/) can be added to pre-mixed fountain solution in steps of 0.25% per litre to stop scumming and keep reverses open.

## Signs your fountain solution is not matched to your water

- calcium deposits building up on rollers
- water settings creeping up to keep the plate clean
- colour that is hard to hold steady through a run

None of these prove the fountain solution is the cause on their own, but they are a good reason to review the grade and dilution.

Repeat jobs are common in every print shop. Keeping the plates from the last run, instead of making new ones, saves plates, time and processing. But a stored plate is only useful if it comes back to press clean and printing as it did before. That depends on how it was gummed, stored and cleaned.

## Why plates need protecting

The non-image areas of an offset plate are designed to hold water and reject ink. Left unprotected, they are exposed to air, handling and dirt. They can oxidise, pick up fingerprints and scratches, and start to accept ink where they should not. Plate gum forms a protective film over the plate that guards those areas until the plate is used again.

## Step 1: clean the plate

Before gumming, remove the ink from the plate.

- Stop the press and shake the plate cleaner container well.
- Sheet off excess ink.
- Apply the cleaner with a water-dampened sponge or cloth, then wipe off with a clean rag.

Choose a cleaner to match the plate's condition. Our [plate cleaner range](../../products/plate-cleaner/) includes:

- **PJNT-1**, an extra-strength alkaline cleaner for scratches, scum and oxidation marks
- **PJNT-2**, a heavy-duty cleaner for UV and conventional inks
- **PJNT-3**, a general cleaner for positive and negative aluminium plates, which also works as a starter for new and stored plates
- **PJNT-4**, an economical cleaner and scratch remover with no abrasives

For short stops, our plate cleaners can also protect the plate: apply as above and buff dry once all ink is removed. The film preserves the plate until its next run.

## Step 2: gum the plate

For storage beyond a short stop, gum the plate.

- Pour a generous amount of [plate gum](../../products/plate-gum/) onto a damp sponge or shop towel.
- Wipe it evenly over the whole plate, so no area is left uncovered.
- Dilute if needed.

If you gum plates in a processor instead, dilute the gum 1 part to 1 part water.

Our plate gum is made from pure gum arabic and chemical gum powders, blended with purified, micro-filtered water. It is safe on all plates and has been tested on most brand-name metal plates.

## Step 3: store the plate

Good storage keeps the gum film intact:

- let the gum dry before stacking or hanging plates
- store plates flat or hanging, so they are not bent or creased
- keep them dry and away from direct sunlight and dust
- separate stacked plates with clean interleaving paper so they do not scratch each other
- label each plate with the job and colour, so it can be found quickly

## Step 4: bring the plate back to press

A stored plate needs its preserving gum and any residual ink removed without damaging the image area. Use a plate cleaner formulated for the job. PJNT-3 works as a starter for new and stored plates, and our plate cleaners lubricate the plate for fast roll-up on press.

## Treating metering rollers with gum

Plate gum is also useful on metal metering rollers. Clean the ink build-up off the roller, then apply gum with a clean sponge or towel, working side to side until the whole roller is treated. Let it dry before restarting the press.

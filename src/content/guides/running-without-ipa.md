For many years, isopropyl alcohol (IPA) has been added to the dampening system of offset presses. It lowers the surface tension of the water so it spreads as a thinner, more even film across the plate. Many printers now want to reduce or remove it, and alcohol-free fountain solutions make that possible.

## Why printers are reducing IPA

- **Fire safety.** IPA is highly flammable. Less alcohol in the pressroom means less flammable liquid stored and handled near the presses.
- **Emissions and air quality.** Alcohol evaporates from the dampening system into the pressroom. Removing it reduces the volatile organic compounds (VOCs) released during printing.
- **Consistency.** Because alcohol evaporates, its concentration in the working solution changes during a run and has to be replenished. A dampening solution without alcohol is easier to keep stable.

## Planning the switch

Moving to alcohol-free dampening works best as a planned change rather than a swap on a busy day.

1. **Start with a clean system.** Drain and clean the dampening tank, lines and rollers before filling with the new solution, so old residue does not affect the first runs.
2. **Check your rollers.** Without alcohol, dampening depends more on the condition and setting of the rollers. Worn, glazed or badly set rollers are worth dealing with first.
3. **Use the recommended dilution.** Our technical team can give you a starting dilution for your press, plates and water.
4. **Run a known job first.** Compare against a job you print often, so you can judge water settings, colour and drying against something familiar.
5. **Keep notes.** Record settings that work for each press, so the whole team runs the new solution the same way.

Not every press will be ready to remove alcohol completely on day one. Some printers reduce IPA in steps first. We can advise on the approach that suits your presses.
